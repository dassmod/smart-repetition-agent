# Extraction and Strength

## Two different numbers, constantly confused

Ask someone what makes coffee "strong" and they'll usually say "more coffee grounds." That's half right, but it conflates two genuinely separate measurements: **strength** and **extraction**.

**Strength** is how much dissolved coffee is in your cup, measured as Total Dissolved Solids (TDS), a percentage. A typical drip coffee is around 1.2-1.4% TDS: for every 100 grams of liquid, 1.2-1.4 grams is actually dissolved coffee, the rest is water.

**Extraction** is a different question entirely: of all the soluble material inside the coffee grounds, what percentage actually dissolved into the water? This is usually expressed as Extraction Yield, and the sweet spot most brewers aim for is 18-22%.

## Why they're independent

Here's the part that trips people up: you can raise strength without touching extraction, and vice versa. If you use more grounds with the same water and the same brew time, you get more dissolved solids (higher strength), but each individual ground particle gave up roughly the same fraction of itself (extraction stays similar). If instead you brew the same amount of grounds for longer, water has more time to pull soluble compounds out of each particle, so extraction rises, independent of how many grounds you started with.

Think of a tea bag. Steep one bag for 1 minute versus 10 minutes in the same cup of water: the extraction is very different (more compounds pulled out over time), but if you'd used two tea bags for that same 1 minute, the STRENGTH would go up while extraction per bag stays about the same. Same idea with coffee.

## Under-extraction and over-extraction

Coffee isn't one compound, it's hundreds, and they dissolve at different rates. The pleasant ones (sugars, some acids) dissolve fastest. The bitter, harsh ones (certain phenolics) dissolve slowest and need more time or heat to come out.

**Under-extracted** coffee (below ~18%) tastes sour and thin, because you've only pulled out the fast-dissolving compounds and stopped before the balancing sweetness fully developed.

**Over-extracted** coffee (above ~22%) tastes bitter and harsh, because you've now pulled out the slow-dissolving bitter compounds too, and they're overwhelming everything else.

The 18-22% window isn't arbitrary. It's the range where you've pulled out enough of the good stuff to taste balanced, without yet pulling out enough of the harsh compounds to taste bitter.
